## What the task is

A kinematic white **cabinet** (57 × 34 × 30 cm) stands on the floor. Its roof has a square **well**
opening (13 × 13 cm); the black bowl starts ~13 cm DOWN the well, standing on a free **elevator
platform** whose cylindrical foot rests on a runner in a low **tunnel** through the cabinet base. A
long free **wedge ram** (1.6 kg) sticks out of the tunnel mouth on a polished rail, ending in a tall
orange **push paddle**. Pushing the paddle horizontally drives the ram's 40° wedge face under the
elevator's foot and converts the horizontal travel into vertical lift (mechanical advantage
≈ 1/tan 40°); at full insertion the paddle seats against the cabinet lintel (the built-in stop), the
foot rests on the ram's FLAT crest (zero back-drive — the lift **holds itself** with hands off), and
the platform stands ~4 mm proud of the roof with the bowl fully exposed. The goal:

> "Drive the wedge ram home to jack the bowl up its well, then slide the bowl backward off the
> proud platform onto the cabinet roof."

`success()` = bowl upright at rest on the rooftop landing area (fixture-frame band behind the well)
∧ everything settled ∧ the bowl actually RODE the lift through the raised position (order-aware
latch). A maroon bottle on the floor is a distractor. Pulling the ram OUT instead only drops the
platform a few millimetres onto the rail and achieves nothing (proven by smoke negative D).

## Strategic difference — vs the seed and vs every corpus neighbor

- **vs the seed:** the seed is one grasp and one free-space carry. Here the bowl is *impossible to
  grasp or lift* (see embodiment) and starts INSIDE the destination fixture, below its surface; the
  only route is a force-multiplying machine — horizontal push converted to vertical elevation by a
  wedge, with a self-holding dwell — followed by a planar slide. The bowl is never carried and never
  leaves contact support.
- **vs i61 cart_ferry (same LIBERO family):** there the destination is reached by *repositioning a
  vehicle* and driving the cargo home aboard it; the cargo moves horizontally throughout. Here the
  destination is static and the core mechanism is *vertical* travel generated by mechanical
  advantage: the pushed body (ram) never carries the bowl, it jacks a third body (the platform)
  through the roof plane from below.
- **vs i306 hatch_shelf / i5 counterweight_shelf:** those gate ACCESS to a static shelf; placement is
  still transport. Here elevation itself is manufactured by the mechanism, and the self-locking crest
  dwell (zero back-drive at full stroke) is load-bearing: phase 2 happens hands-off on the lift.
- **vs i3 tunnel_shuttle / i19 moat_causeway:** those push cargo (or a carrier) along a horizontal
  route; no lift, no wedge, no order-gated elevation.
- **vs i57 hanoi_rings / i48 cask_weight_sort / i27 bell-herd:** no ordering/sorting/multi-agent
  component; this is a two-stage mechanism chain where stage 1 physically creates the support plane
  stage 2 slides across.
- **vs robobench suite (balance_scale, combination_safe, syringe, pen_holder):** no articulated
  joint anywhere — the jack is emergent from rigid contact (wedge + roller foot + guides).

No task in the read corpus converts a horizontal push into vertical cargo elevation via a wedge with
a self-holding dwell, and none starts the cargo *inside and below* its own destination surface.

## Rubric (latched credit, `score()`)

| latch | condition (fixture frame, every physics substep) | weight |
|---|---|---|
| `_partlift_ever` | bowl aboard ∧ platform top risen ≥ ~45 mm | 0.15 |
| `_raised_ever` | bowl aboard ∧ platform top in the raised (roof-level) band | 0.25 |
| `_landed_ever` | `_raised_ever` ∧ bowl upright in the roof landing band (**order-gated**) | 0.30 |

`score = Σ weights`, capped at 0.95 unless `success()`, which returns exactly 1.0. The landed latch
is gated on the raised latch, so the seed's strategy (bowl placed straight onto the roof) scores
**0** — proven by smoke negative A/A2: even raising the empty elevator afterwards earns nothing
(raised credit requires the bowl aboard).

## Solution (`solve.py`) — ZERO teleports

All interaction is pulsed external force + contact dynamics (the ram's travel and the bowl's slide
ARE the mechanism, so teleporting would skip it):

1. **RAM** — pulsed 25 N push on the ram along the fixture's −x (force on only below 0.10 m/s; the
   back-drive load on the slope is only ~5–7 N). The foot rides the 40° face, the platform climbs
   ~13.5 cm, the paddle takes a soft seating impact at the lintel, and the foot lands on the flat
   crest. Release, settle: the lift holds with the force off. → 0.400
2. **SLIDE** — gentle pulsed 1.5 N push on the bowl along −x (cap 0.08 m/s; sliding needs ~0.7 N,
   and 1.5 N at rim height cannot tip the bowl): it slides off the 4 mm-proud platform (a small step
   DOWN) onto the roof and creeps to mid-landing-area; friction stops it. Release, settle. → 1.000
3. **PERSISTENCE** — ≥ 3.5 simulated seconds fully hands-off; `success()` must still hold before
   `SIM_GEN_SOLVE: SUCCESS` is printed.

On these pods the default external-force call applies the wrench in the body's CURRENT frame, so
`drive()` pre-encodes every command per step with `quat_apply_inverse(q_now, f_world)` (correct for
the bowl's free spawn yaw) and keeps a raw-world fallback behind a measured-progress stall probe.

Verified on the forge: seeds 0, 1, 2 all print 0.000 → 0.400 → 1.000 → `SIM_GEN_SOLVE: SUCCESS`,
rc = 0.

## Embodiment argument (single Franka, 8 cm parallel jaw, OSC)

The same plan executes with one arm, and **no stage admits a shortcut by grasping the bowl**:

- **Bowl cannot be grasped or lifted.** Outer diameter 11.6 cm > 8 cm jaw span — no side or rim
  straddle anywhere, ever. Down the well the geometry is worse: the well is 13 cm square, the gap
  between bowl wall and well wall is ~7 mm (< a fingertip), and the rim sits ~6.9 cm below the roof
  — deeper than the fingers (~5.4 cm), so no top-down rim pinch either. The bowl can only leave the
  well by riding the elevator.
- **Ram stage** = palm-push: the orange paddle is an 8.4 × 24 cm board standing at z ≤ 26 cm,
  outside the cabinet — a flat-hand horizontal push; the lintel stop terminates the stroke, so no
  precise servoing is needed, and the ~25 N stroke is well within arm capability.
- **Slide stage** = fingertip drag: after the lift the bowl stands proud of the open roof; place one
  fingertip behind its far wall and drag it backward onto the roof (a planar ~1–2 N drag, with a
  4 mm step DOWN off the platform — nothing to lift).
- **One plausible base pose:** base at fixture ≈ (x = +0.15 m, y = −0.55 m), facing the cabinet.
  From there the paddle face (x ≈ 0.49 → 0.22 m, z ≤ 0.26 m), the exposed bowl at the well
  (x ≈ 0.05, z ≈ 0.33 m) and the landing area (x ≈ −0.15, z ≈ 0.33 m) are all within ~0.8 m reach,
  with the lane of the paddle stroke clear of the base.

## Execution order (declared)

Strictly sequential; each stage gates the next physically AND in the rubric:

1. Ram the wedge home (without the lift the bowl is unreachable at the bottom of the well; smoke
   negative D shows pulling the ram out achieves nothing).
2. Slide the bowl off the platform (the landed latch only fires after the raised latch — roof
   credit cannot be earned by placing the bowl first, smoke negatives A/A2).
3. Hands-off persistence (the crest dwell holds the platform; the terminal state is fully passive
   on static roof).
